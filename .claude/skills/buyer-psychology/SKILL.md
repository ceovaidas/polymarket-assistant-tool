# Buyer psychology analysis

Reply in Lithuanian. Run this before writing copy, hooks or offers for a product, and whenever the user
asks "will this sell?". Base every claim on something real: the product, its reviews, comments under
similar viral videos, competitor listings, Shopify data. Mark guesses as guesses.

## 1. Two people, not one

Jolly Haul sells gifts, so separate them:
- **Buyer** (pays): who they are, age, relation to the recipient, budget, when they shop, what they fear
  (giving a boring gift, being late for Christmas, wasting money).
- **Recipient** (uses it): who they are and the moment the gift lands (unwrapping, the room's reaction).
Most copy should talk to the buyer about the recipient's reaction.

## 2. Mind map of the buyer

| Question | What to write down |
|---|---|
| What they think now | The sentence in their head ("Dad has everything", "I always give socks") |
| Pain | What is uncomfortable about it, made concrete (the polite "thanks" face, last-minute panic) |
| Desire / end result | The vivid picture they want (whole table laughing, the photo everyone shares) |
| Expectations | Delivery time, quality, sizing, returns, how it looks in real life vs the ad |
| Objections | "Will it arrive before Christmas?", "Is it cheap quality?", "Will he actually wear it?", "Size?" |
| Trigger moments | Black Friday, Dec cut-off dates, office Secret Santa, a viral video they just watched |
| Words they use | Copy phrases from real comments and reviews, not marketing language |

## 3. Will they buy? Score 1-5 each

- **Solves a real problem or creates a real moment?** (gift panic solved, guaranteed laugh)
- **Instant "I know who this is for"** (a specific person comes to mind in 2 seconds)
- **Visual / demonstrable in 3 seconds of video**
- **Price inside the impulse range** for the buyer (see 4)
- **Shareable** (people tag someone in the comments)
- **Delivery fits the date** they need it
Total under 18/30 → say honestly that the product is weak and what would fix it (bundle, angle, price).

## 4. How much will they spend

- Impulse gift (one tap from a video): roughly $20-45. Over ~$50 needs more proof (reviews, video, bundle).
- Find the price anchor the buyer already has (similar items on Amazon/Etsy/TikTok Shop) and price
  against it; end in .99.
- Average order value ideas: 2-pack ("99% off the second one"), matching family set, add-on under $10
  in the cart, $0 shipping threshold just above the typical cart.

## 5. Selling rules (from the user's playbook)

1. **Problem:** make the pain point vivid and uncomfortable (true, specific, relatable).
2. **Transparency:** show the real product, real delivery dates, real reviews, honest sizing.
3. **Low ticket → push the sale** (clear CTA, urgency from real deadlines). High ticket → ask questions
   that make them admit the need (quiz: "Who is it for? What does he already have?").
4. **Cost of inaction:** show what not buying costs (another forgettable gift, missing the Christmas
   delivery cut-off) as bigger than the price.
5. **Tonality:** speak with confidence and authority, short sentences, no "maybe".
6. **Sell the end result:** paint the moment (unwrapping, the laugh, the family photo).
7. **Extra mile / humour:** be funny and familiar; the brand voice should feel like a friend.
Plus the offer wording table and the psychology rules in `CLAUDE.md`.

## 6. Boundaries

Pain points and motivations of the typical buyer are fair game. Do not target people in a vulnerable
state (debt, grief, addiction, minors), do not invent fears, reviews, scarcity or claims, and do not
promise what the product cannot do. These get the store, the ads account and payouts shut down.

## 7. Output

1. Buyer + recipient profile (short).
2. The mind-map table.
3. Score table with total and a verdict: sell / sell with changes / skip.
4. Price and AOV recommendation.
5. Top 5 hooks and 3 product-page headlines built on the pain → end-result arc, following the offer
   wording rules.
6. The 3 biggest objections and the exact line or page element that answers each.
